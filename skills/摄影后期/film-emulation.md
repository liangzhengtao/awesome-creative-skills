# Film Emulation

> Category: 摄影后期 (Photo Post-processing) | Style: Analog Film Photography | Last updated: 2026

## When to Use

Emulate the distinctive look of analog film stocks in digital photography. Perfect for:

- Portrait photography with warm, flattering skin tones
- Street photography with documentary authenticity
- Landscape photography with rich, organic color
- Wedding and lifestyle photography
- Fashion editorial with vintage character
- Personal projects seeking analog warmth and nostalgia
- Building consistent preset collections

## Design Philosophy

Film photography created its magic through chemical processes — each film stock had a unique personality born from its emulsion chemistry, grain structure, and color response curves. Digital emulation isn't just adding grain and shifting colors; it's about capturing the *character*:

- **Organic imperfection** — Film grain is random and textured, not uniform noise
- **Color science** — Each stock responds differently to light; skin tones, skies, and greens shift uniquely
- **Dynamic range behavior** — Film compresses highlights gently and rolls off shadows softly
- **Halation** — Bright light sources bloom with a warm glow (especially in cinema stocks)
- **Response to light** — Film handles overexposure more gracefully than digital

## Decision Priority

1. **Analyze the scene** — Lighting conditions, dominant colors, subject matter
2. **Select the film stock** — Match the stock's character to the scene's needs
3. **Apply grain structure** — Appropriate size and intensity for the chosen stock
4. **Adjust color curves** — Per-channel curves matching the film's response
5. **Fine-tune response** — Highlight rolloff, shadow compression, color crossover

## Prompt Templates

### Kodak Portra 160/400

```
Emulate Kodak Portra [160/400] film look on this photograph. Apply warm,
flattering color science with slightly lifted shadows (not crushed black),
gentle highlight rolloff with no harsh clipping, warm yellow-green color
shift in midtones, beautiful skin tone rendering with peachy-warm quality,
fine grain structure at ISO [160/400] density, slight reduction in overall
saturation especially in blues and greens, soft contrast curve with rich
midtone detail, very slight warm cast in highlights, natural color crossover
in shadows leaning toward teal-blue. The result should look like it was shot
on medium format film with beautiful natural light.
```

**Characteristics:**
- Grain: Fine, smooth, well-controlled
- Colors: Warm, natural, slightly desaturated
- Skin tones: Exceptional — peachy, flattering, luminous
- Highlights: Gentle rolloff, never harsh
- Shadows: Lifted, never truly black
- Best for: Portraits, weddings, fashion, lifestyle

### Fuji Velvia 50/100

```
Emulate Fuji Velvia [50/100] film look on this landscape photograph. Apply
vivid, saturated color rendering with deep rich greens, intense blue skies,
and warm golden highlights. Strong contrast with deep blacks and bright
highlights, fine grain at [50/100] ISO, pushed saturation especially in
reds, greens, and blues, slight warm shift in golden hour light, cool shift
in blue hour, punchy midtones with high visual impact. The result should look
like a transparency slide viewed on a light box — rich, saturated, and
three-dimensional.
```

**Characteristics:**
- Grain: Ultra-fine (especially Velvia 50)
- Colors: Highly saturated, vivid, punchy
- Contrast: High, with deep blacks
- Skies: Intense cobalt blue
- Greens: Rich emerald, almost hyper-real
- Best for: Landscapes, nature, travel, architecture

### Ilford HP5 Plus 400

```
Emulate Ilford HP5 Plus 400 black and white film look on this photograph.
Convert to monochrome with classic panchromatic response, medium-fine grain
structure with visible but pleasing texture, full tonal range from deep rich
blacks to clean whites, slightly lifted midtones creating a characteristic
"film look", natural contrast that can handle push processing, beautiful
gray scale separation especially in skin tones, slight grain clumping in
shadow areas typical of silver halide chemistry. The result should look like
it was developed in a darkroom with D-76 developer.
```

**Characteristics:**
- Grain: Medium, classic silver halide texture
- Tonal range: Full, with beautiful midtone separation
- Blacks: Rich but not crushed
- Whites: Clean, not blown
- Push capability: Handles +1 to +3 stops well with increasing grain
- Best for: Street photography, documentary, portraits, general purpose

### Polaroid 600 / SX-70

```
Emulate Polaroid [600/SX-70] instant film look on this photograph. Apply
slightly soft focus with warm color shift, characteristic color palette of
muted pastels with warm yellow-green cast, low contrast with lifted blacks
and soft highlights, instant film frame border with white margin and bottom
text area, slight color fading especially in highlights, dreamy nostalgic
quality, characteristic chemical development artifacts — slight unevenness
in color saturation, mild Newton rings or roller marks, the unique look of
dye diffusion transfer process.
```

**Characteristics:**
- Grain: Soft, diffused, not sharp
- Colors: Muted pastels, warm cast, slightly faded
- Contrast: Low, dreamy
- Focus: Slightly soft overall
- Frame: Iconic white border with bottom text area
- Best for: Casual, nostalgic, artistic, whimsical subjects

### Daguerreotype (1840s)

```
Emulate Daguerreotype process look on this photograph. Convert to monochrome
with extreme contrast — mirror-like silver highlights against deep black
shadows, characteristic vignetting darkening toward edges, shallow depth of
field effect (even in focus areas appear slightly soft), specular highlights
creating mirror-like reflections on silver plate, visible surface texture of
polished copper plate, slight oxidation marks and chemical artifacts at edges,
one-of-a-kind unique quality — each daguerreotype is an original object.
The result should look like a museum-quality 19th century photographic plate.
```

**Characteristics:**
- Tone: Silvery, mirror-like quality
- Contrast: Extreme — deep shadows, bright highlights
- Vignetting: Heavy, natural optical vignette
- Detail: Incredible resolution where in focus
- Surface: Polished metal plate appearance
- Best for: Artistic portraits, still life, conceptual work

### Grain Structure Guide

| Film Speed | Grain Size | Grain Character |
|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| ISO 50-100 | Barely visible | Smooth, almost digital |
| ISO 200-400 | Fine, pleasant | Classic "film look" |
| ISO 800-1600 | Medium, present | Artistic, tactile |
| ISO 3200+ | Coarse, prominent | Gritty, photojournalistic |

## Common Patterns

1. **The golden hour glow** — Portra 400 + golden hour light = warm, luminous skin tones
2. **The moody street scene** — Tri-X 400 + high contrast = gritty urban documentary
3. **The dreamy travel** — Fuji Pro 400H + soft light = pastel travel diary
4. **The vivid landscape** — Velvia 50 + dramatic sky = hyper-real nature
5. **The nostalgic family** — Kodak Gold 200 + flash = warm family album memories
6. **The cinematic night** — Cinestill 800T + neon lights = halation-drenched cityscapes

## Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Uniform grain overlay** — Real film grain varies with exposure; brighter areas show less grain
- **Ignoring the base curve** — Color shifts alone don't emulate film; the tonal curve matters most
- **Over-saturation** — Most portrait stocks are actually slightly desaturated
- **Crushing blacks** — Film rarely produces true black; shadows have detail and color
- **Missing highlight rolloff** — The soft compression of highlights is key to the film look
- **Wrong grain for the speed** — ISO 100 film should not have ISO 3200 grain
- **Applying to heavily processed images** — Start from a relatively neutral base for best results
- **Forgetting halation** — Cinestill and cinema stocks have distinctive halation around bright sources
- **One-size-fits-all** — Different scenes need different stocks; a landscape stock hurts portraits

## 中文版本

### 使用场景

适用于在数码照片上模拟经典胶片的独特质感：

- 人像摄影追求温暖自然的肤色
- 街头摄影的纪实真实感
- 风光摄影的浓郁有机色彩
- 婚礼和生活方式摄影
- 时尚编辑的复古质感
- 追求模拟温暖和怀旧感的个人项目
- 建立统一的预设（preset）集合

### 核心步骤

1. **分析场景** — 光线条件（日光/钨丝灯/阴天/黄金时段/棚拍）、主体类型、当前色温和曝光
2. **选择胶片型号** — 根据场景需求从胶片对照表中匹配（如 Portra 适合人像、Velvia 适合风光）
3. **构建处理 Prompt** — 组合胶片型号、颗粒描述、暗部/亮部色调、对比度、饱和度、肤色方向、特殊特征
4. **输出技术调整** — 针对目标软件给出具体操作步骤

### 模板说明

| 胶片 | 色彩风格 | 颗粒 | 最佳用途 |
|------|---------|------|---------|
| **Kodak Portra 160/400** | 温暖自然，桃粉肤色 | 细腻 | 人像、婚礼、时尚 |
| **Fuji Velvia 50/100** | 高饱和，翠绿+钴蓝 | 超细 | 风光、自然、建筑 |
| **Ilford HP5 Plus 400** | 经典黑白，中间调丰富 | 中等 | 街头、纪实、通用黑白 |
| **Polaroid 600 / SX-70** | 柔和粉彩，暖调，梦幻 | 柔散 | 休闲、怀旧、艺术 |
| **Daguerreotype (1840s)** | 银版镜面，极端对比 | 无颗粒 | 艺术人像、概念摄影 |

每款胶片附带色彩响应曲线说明（红/绿/蓝通道在暗部、中间调、亮部的偏移方向）。

### 常见陷阱

- **均匀颗粒叠加** — 真实胶片颗粒随曝光变化，亮部颗粒更少
- **忽略基础曲线** — 仅偏移颜色不够，影调曲线才是胶片质感的关键
- **过度饱和** — 大多人像胶片实际上是略去饱和的
- **压黑** — 胶片很少产生纯黑，暗部保留有细节和色彩
- **忽略亮部过渡** — 亮部的柔和压缩是胶片感的核心
- **ISO 与颗粒不匹配** — ISO 100 的胶片不应有 ISO 3200 的颗粒
- **用于重度后期的图片** — 应从相对中性的原片开始处理效果最佳
- **忽略光晕（Halation）** — Cinestill 和电影胶片在亮光源周围有独特光晕
- **一刀切** — 不同场景需要不同胶片型号；风光胶片不适合拍人像
