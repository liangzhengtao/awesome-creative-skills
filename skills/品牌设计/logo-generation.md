# Logo Generation

> Category: 品牌设计 (Brand Design) | Style: Multi-Style Logo Design | Last updated: 2026

## When to Use

Generate professional logos from brand descriptions, keywords, and visual preferences. Perfect for:

- Startup founders needing initial brand identity
- Small businesses refreshing their visual presence
- Side projects and personal brands
- Product naming and visual brainstorming
- Design exploration before hiring a professional designer
- Event and conference branding
- App icons and favicon generation

## Design Philosophy

A great logo is the simplest possible expression of a brand's identity. It must work everywhere — from a favicon to a billboard:

- **Simplicity first** — If it can't be drawn from memory, it's too complex
- **Meaningful** — Every shape, color, and type choice should connect to the brand
- **Versatile** — Must work in one color, at any size, on any background
- **Timeless** — Avoid trends that will date quickly
- **Distinctive** — Must stand out among competitors and be ownable
- **Appropriate** — A law firm and a toy company need very different logos

## Decision Priority

1. **Understand brand values** — What does the brand stand for? Who is the audience?
2. **Select logo style** — Wordmark, lettermark, pictorial, abstract, mascot, combination
3. **Design the symbol/mark** — The visual element that represents the brand
4. **Choose typography** — Font that expresses brand personality
5. **Select color palette** — Colors that evoke the right emotions

## Instructions for AI Assistant

### Step 1: Gather Brand Information
Collect or infer:
- Brand name and tagline
- Industry and target audience
- Brand personality (3-5 adjectives)
- Competitor landscape
- Preferred style direction

### Step 2: Select Logo Type
| Type | Description | Best For |
|------|-------------|----------|
| Wordmark | Text-only (Google, Coca-Cola) | Distinctive names |
| Lettermark | Initials (IBM, HBO) | Long names |
| Pictorial | Recognizable icon (Apple, Twitter) | Universal concepts |
| Abstract | Geometric form (Nike swoosh, Pepsi) | Unique positioning |
| Mascot | Character (KFC, Pringles) | Family-friendly brands |
| Combination | Mark + text (Burger King, Lacoste) | Most versatile choice |
| Emblem | Text inside symbol (Starbucks, Harley) | Traditional, established |

### Step 3: Build the Prompt
```
Base: "[LOGO TYPE] logo design for [BRAND NAME], [INDUSTRY], style: [STYLE],
[SHAPE/ICON DESCRIPTION], typography: [FONT STYLE], color palette: [COLORS],
[RENDERING: flat/vector/3D/minimal], [APPLICATION: versatile/one-color/icon],
white background, professional quality, [SPECIAL NOTES]"
```

### Step 4: Review and Iterate
Check against the Logo Design Principles below.

## Style Reference

### Logo Design Principles

1. **The Squint Test** — Squint at the logo; if you can still recognize it, the shapes are strong enough
2. **The Napkin Sketch** — Can you draw it quickly on a napkin? If not, simplify
3. **The One-Color Test** — Does it work in pure black on white? If yes, the form is strong
4. **The Size Test** — Does it work at 16×16px favicon AND on a billboard?
5. **The Time Test** — Will this look dated in 5 years? Avoid trend-dependent elements

## Common Patterns

1. **Negative space play** — Hidden meaning in the counter-space of letters (FedEx arrow)
2. **Geometric construction** — Built from circles, squares, triangles with mathematical precision
3. **Monogram fusion** — Two or more letters merged into a single mark
4. **Icon + Wordmark** — Separate mark that can work alone or with text
5. **Responsive logo** — Full version → simplified → icon-only for different sizes
6. **Color system** — Primary logo + secondary color variations for different backgrounds

## Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Too many ideas** — A logo should express ONE concept clearly, not five concepts poorly
- **Clip art aesthetics** — Generic symbols (globe, handshake, lightbulb) feel cheap
- **Trend dependence** — Avoid swooshes, bevels, gradients that age poorly
- **Complexity at small sizes** — Fine details disappear in favicons and app icons
- **Color dependence** — Must work in one color; color is enhancement, not structure
- **Font overload** — One typeface family maximum; two weights is usually enough
- **Literal illustration** — A restaurant doesn't need a literal plate and fork
- **Ignoring competitors** — The logo must stand out in its specific market
- **Missing the brief** — A beautiful logo that doesn't fit the brand is a failed logo
- **Not testing applications** — Mock up on business cards, websites, signage, merchandise
